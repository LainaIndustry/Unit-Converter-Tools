Metric vs Imperial System: Understanding the Main Differences

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 8 min read

The metric and imperial systems are the two main measurement systems used around the world. While the metric system is the international standard, the imperial system is still widely used in the United States and a few other countries.

What is the Metric System?

The metric system, also known as the International System of Units (SI), is a decimal-based system of measurement. It was developed in France in the late 18th century and has since been adopted by most countries worldwide.

  • Based on multiples of 10 - easy conversions
  • Standard prefixes: kilo, centi, milli, etc.
  • Seven base units: meter, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela
  • Used by: Most countries worldwide, scientific community

What is the Imperial System?

The imperial system, also called the British Imperial system, evolved from earlier English units. It was formalized in 1824 and is still used in the United States, Liberia, and Myanmar.

  • Based on historical units - less consistent relationships
  • Units: inches, feet, yards, miles, pounds, gallons
  • Used by: United States, Liberia, Myanmar

Common Conversions

  • Length: 1 meter = 3.28084 feet
  • Weight: 1 kilogram = 2.20462 pounds
  • Volume: 1 liter = 0.264172 gallons
  • Temperature: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32

Which System Should You Use?

  • Scientific work: Metric system (SI) is the international standard
  • Everyday use in the US: Imperial system is common
  • International communication: Metric is preferred
  • Cooking: Both systems are used depending on the recipe source

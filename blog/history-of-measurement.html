The History of Measurement: From Ancient Times to SI

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 10 min read

Measurement is one of humanity's oldest and most essential practices. From the earliest civilizations to the modern age, the need to quantify the world around us has driven innovation in science, trade, and everyday life.

Ancient Measurements

The earliest measurements were based on the human body. The cubit, for example, was the length of a forearm from elbow to fingertip. The foot was based on the length of a human foot. These body-based units varied from person to person, leading to inconsistencies.

  • Egyptian cubit: Used to build the pyramids
  • Roman foot: The basis for the modern foot
  • Greek stadion: Used to measure distance in athletics

The Development of Standardization

As trade expanded, the need for standardized measurements became critical. Rulers and governments began to define official units to ensure fairness in commerce.

  • Magna Carta (1215): Included provisions for standard measures
  • British Imperial System (1824): Formalized British units
  • Metric System (1795): Developed in France during the Revolution

The International System of Units (SI)

Today, the International System of Units (SI) is the global standard for measurement. It defines seven base units from which all other units are derived:

  • Meter (m): Length
  • Kilogram (kg): Mass
  • Second (s): Time
  • Ampere (A): Electric current
  • Kelvin (K): Temperature
  • Mole (mol): Amount of substance
  • Candela (cd): Luminous intensity

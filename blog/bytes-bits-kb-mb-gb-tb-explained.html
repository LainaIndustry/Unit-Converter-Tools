Bytes, Bits, KB, MB, GB and TB Explained

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 7 min read

Digital storage can be confusing with all the different units: bits, bytes, kilobytes, megabytes, gigabytes, and terabytes. This guide will explain what each unit means and how they relate to each other.

The Basics: Bits and Bytes

Bit (b): The smallest unit of digital data. A bit can have only two values: 0 or 1.

Byte (B): A group of 8 bits. One byte can represent 256 different values (2⁸).

  • 1 bit = 0 or 1
  • 1 byte = 8 bits
  • 1 byte can store one character (like 'A')

Decimal vs Binary Prefixes

There are two systems for measuring digital storage: decimal (base 10) and binary (base 2).

Decimal (Base 10) - Used by storage manufacturers

  • 1 KB = 1000 bytes
  • 1 MB = 1000 KB
  • 1 GB = 1000 MB
  • 1 TB = 1000 GB

Binary (Base 2) - Used by computer systems

  • 1 KiB = 1024 bytes
  • 1 MiB = 1024 KiB
  • 1 GiB = 1024 MiB
  • 1 TiB = 1024 GiB

Practical Examples

  • A text message: ~1 KB
  • A typical photo: 2-5 MB
  • A song (MP3): 3-5 MB
  • A movie (HD): 4-8 GB
  • A smartphone: 64-512 GB
  • A laptop: 256 GB - 2 TB

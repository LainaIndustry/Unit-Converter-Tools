Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin Explained

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 6 min read

Temperature is one of the most fundamental measurements in science and everyday life. But did you know there are three main temperature scales used around the world? Let's explore Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin in detail.

Celsius (°C)

The Celsius scale is the most widely used temperature scale in the world. It was developed by Swedish astronomer Anders Celsius in 1742.

  • Freezing point of water: 0°C
  • Boiling point of water: 100°C
  • Used by: Most countries worldwide, scientific community

Fahrenheit (°F)

The Fahrenheit scale was developed by German physicist Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit in 1724. It is primarily used in the United States and a few other countries.

  • Freezing point of water: 32°F
  • Boiling point of water: 212°F
  • Used by: United States, Belize, Cayman Islands

Kelvin (K)

The Kelvin scale is the SI base unit for temperature. It was developed by William Thomson, Lord Kelvin, in 1848. Unlike Celsius and Fahrenheit, Kelvin does not use degrees.

  • Absolute zero: 0 K
  • Freezing point of water: 273.15 K
  • Boiling point of water: 373.15 K
  • Used by: Scientific community worldwide

Quick Reference

  • Room temperature: 20-22°C (68-72°F)
  • Body temperature: 37°C (98.6°F)
  • Water freezes: 0°C (32°F)
  • Water boils: 100°C (212°F)

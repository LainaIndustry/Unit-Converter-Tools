Energy Units Explained: Joules, Calories and Kilowatt Hours

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 5 min read

Energy is a fundamental concept in physics and everyday life. It comes in many forms and is measured using various units depending on the context. This guide explains the most common energy units and how they relate to each other.

What is Energy?

Energy is the capacity to do work or produce change. It exists in many forms including kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, and electrical energy.

Common Energy Units

Joule (J)

The Joule is the SI unit of energy. One Joule is the energy transferred when a force of one Newton moves an object one meter.

  • 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s²
  • Used in: Science and engineering

Calorie (cal) and Kilocalorie (kcal)

Calories are commonly used in nutrition and chemistry. One calorie is the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 gram of water by 1°C.

  • 1 cal = 4.184 J
  • 1 kcal = 1,000 cal = 4,184 J
  • Used in: Nutrition, chemistry, cooking

Note: In nutrition, "Calorie" (with a capital C) typically means kilocalorie (kcal).

British Thermal Unit (BTU)

The BTU is a unit of heat energy used primarily in the heating and cooling industries.

  • 1 BTU = 1,055.06 J
  • Used in: HVAC, heating systems

Watt-hour (Wh) and Kilowatt-hour (kWh)

These units are commonly used to measure electrical energy consumption.

  • 1 Wh = 3,600 J
  • 1 kWh = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 MJ
  • Used in: Electricity billing, battery capacity

  • A typical meal: ~2,000 kcal = 8,368,000 J
  • A LED light bulb: 10 W = 10 J/s
  • Running for 30 minutes: ~300 kcal = 1,255,200 J
  • Monthly electricity: ~1,000 kWh = 3,600,000,000 J

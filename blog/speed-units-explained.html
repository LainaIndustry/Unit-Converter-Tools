Understanding Speed Units: KM/H, MPH and M/S

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 5 min read

Speed is a fundamental measurement that we encounter daily, from driving to sports to scientific applications. Understanding speed units and how to convert between them is essential in many contexts.

What is Speed?

Speed is the rate at which an object covers distance. It's calculated by dividing distance by time.

Speed = Distance / Time

Common Speed Units

Kilometers per Hour (km/h)

The most commonly used speed unit in the world, used for road signs and vehicle speedometers in most countries.

  • 1 km/h = 0.27778 m/s
  • 1 km/h = 0.621371 mph
  • Used in: Most countries for road speed

Miles per Hour (mph)

Used primarily in the United States, the United Kingdom, and a few other countries for road speeds.

  • 1 mph = 1.60934 km/h
  • 1 mph = 0.44704 m/s
  • Used in: US, UK, and a few other countries

Meters per Second (m/s)

The SI unit for speed, used extensively in scientific and engineering applications.

  • 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h
  • 1 m/s = 2.23694 mph
  • Used in: Science, engineering, physics

Knot (kn)

Used in maritime and aviation navigation, defined as one nautical mile per hour.

  • 1 knot = 1.852 km/h
  • 1 knot = 0.514444 m/s
  • Used in: Maritime, aviation, weather

Mach

Used to describe speed relative to the speed of sound. Mach 1 is the speed of sound.

  • Mach 1 ≈ 343 m/s (at sea level)
  • Mach 1 ≈ 1,234.8 km/h
  • Used in: Aviation, aerospace

Real-World Examples

  • Walking speed: ~5 km/h (~3.1 mph)
  • Running speed: ~10 km/h (~6.2 mph)
  • Highway speed: ~100 km/h (~62 mph)
  • Airliner cruise speed: ~900 km/h (~560 mph)
  • Sound speed: ~1,235 km/h (~767 mph) at sea level

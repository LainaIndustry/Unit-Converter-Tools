Understanding Pressure Units: PSI, Bar and Pascal

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 6 min read

Pressure is a fundamental physical quantity that we encounter every day, from the air in our tires to the weather forecast. Understanding pressure units is essential for many applications in science, engineering, and everyday life.

What is Pressure?

Pressure is defined as force per unit area. It measures how much force is applied over a specific surface area.

Pressure = Force / Area

Common Pressure Units

Pascal (Pa)

The Pascal is the SI unit for pressure. One Pascal equals one Newton per square meter (N/m²).

  • 1 Pa = 1 N/m²
  • 1 kPa = 1,000 Pa
  • 1 MPa = 1,000,000 Pa
  • Used in: Scientific and engineering applications

PSI (Pounds per Square Inch)

PSI is widely used in the United States and is common in automotive and industrial applications.

  • 1 PSI = 6,894.76 Pa
  • Used in: Tire pressure, hydraulic systems, air compressors

Bar

The Bar is a metric unit of pressure that is approximately equal to atmospheric pressure at sea level.

  • 1 bar = 100,000 Pa = 100 kPa
  • 1 mbar = 0.001 bar
  • Used in: Meteorology, industrial processes

Atmosphere (atm)

The atmosphere is a unit of pressure equal to standard atmospheric pressure at sea level.

  • 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 101.325 kPa
  • 1 atm = 14.6959 PSI
  • Used in: Chemistry, scuba diving

Torr and mmHg

These units are commonly used in medical and vacuum applications.

  • 1 torr = 133.322 Pa
  • 1 mmHg = 133.322 Pa (approximately equal to 1 torr)
  • Used in: Blood pressure measurements, vacuum systems

  • Weather forecasting: Atmospheric pressure

  • Industry: Hydraulic systems (bar, PSI)

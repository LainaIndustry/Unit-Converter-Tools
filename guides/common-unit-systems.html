Common Unit Systems: Metric, Imperial, and SI

📅 Updated: January 2026 📖 8 min read

There are several measurement systems used around the world. Understanding these systems is essential for international communication, trade, and everyday life.

The Metric System

The metric system is the most widely used measurement system in the world. It is a decimal-based system that uses prefixes to indicate multiples of ten.

  • Base units: Meter (length), gram (mass), liter (volume)
  • Prefixes: kilo (1000), centi (1/100), milli (1/1000)
  • Used by: Most countries worldwide

The Imperial System

The imperial system, also known as the British Imperial system, is primarily used in the United States and a few other countries.

  • Base units: Foot (length), pound (mass), gallon (volume)
  • Subdivisions: Inches, ounces, quarts
  • Used by: United States, Liberia, Myanmar

The International System of Units (SI)

The SI system is the modern form of the metric system. It is the international standard for scientific and technical measurements.

  • Seven base units: Meter, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela
  • Derived units: Newton, joule, watt, pascal
  • Used by: Scientific community worldwide
